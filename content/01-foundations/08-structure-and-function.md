> **Computer Organization and Architecture**

## Structure and Function

- Computer is a complex system.
- It contains millions of elementary electronic components.
- Such a complex system is understood through its hierarchical nature.
- A hierarchical system consists of interrelated subsystems.
- Each subsystem can itself be divided into smaller subsystems.

### Structure

- The way in which the components are interrelated.

### Function

- The operation of each individual component as part of the structure.

## Two Ways to Describe a Complex System

There are two ways to describe a complex system:

- Starting from the bottom and building up to the complete system.
- Starting with a top view and decomposing the system into its subparts.

The book follows the top-down approach:

- Begin with the major components of a computer.
- Describe their structure and function.
- Then proceed to successively lower levels of the hierarchy.

## Four Basic Functions of a Computer

A computer performs four basic functions:

- Data Processing
- Data Storage
- Data Movement
- Control

## Data Processing

- A computer must be able to process data.
- Data may take a wide variety of forms.
- Processing requirements can also vary widely.
- Computer processing consists of fundamental methods or types of data processing.

## Data Storage

- A computer must be able to store data.
- Data being processed may require temporary storage.
- A computer also provides long-term storage.
- Files can be stored for later retrieval and updating.

## Data Movement

- A computer must be able to move data between itself and the outside world.
- External devices can act as sources or destinations of data.
- Data received from or delivered to a directly connected device is called Input/Output (I/O).
- Such a device is called a peripheral.

## Control

- The computer must control its data processing, storage, and movement functions.
- Within the computer, the Control Unit manages computer resources.
- It coordinates the operation of the functional parts of the computer.
- It performs this coordination in response to instructions.
